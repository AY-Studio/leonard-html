The clean break of robotic demolition in confined spaces

Published on 22 January 2026

Introduction

Removing spent refractory from a preheater tower or a boiler throat used to mean sending people into the most hostile place on site — confined, hot, and structurally unpredictable. Remote robotic demolition changes that calculus entirely. This article looks at how a clean break is planned, executed, and verified, and why it consistently beats manual methods on both safety and schedule.

The principle is simple: keep the crew out of the vessel and let the machine take the punishment. What a controlled break involves

Every scope is different, but the sequence is broadly the same. An unordered list captures the phases:

  • Survey the lining and map the anchor pattern.
  • Stage the arm and establish exclusion zones — no personnel past the barrier.
  • Break down the lining in sequence, protecting the shell beneath.
  • Clear, inspect, and hand back a verified surface.

And the decision criteria, in priority order:

  1. Can we remove people from the hot zone entirely?
  2. Will the shell be protected throughout?
  3. Does the method shorten the critical path?

Conclusion

The clean break is not just a safer way to demolish — it is a faster, more predictable one. By removing people from the vessel and letting a remotely operated arm do the work, plants recover thermal efficiency sooner and hand a verified surface back to the reline crew.
